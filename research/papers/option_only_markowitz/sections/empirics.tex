
\section{Empirical Verification}

\subsection{Design}

The empirical exercise has two layers. The equity-option layer uses monthly rolling option
buckets on AAPL, AMZN, GOOGL, JPM, META, MSFT, NVDA, and TSLA. Each date-bucket
representative is the liquid contract with the highest volume and acceptable relative
spread in one of four buckets: ATM, near put, near call, and put wing. The VIX-option
layer is built separately from raw Databento OPRA VIX chain shards. It preserves contract
identity, parses OSI expiry/right/strike, aligns each contract to the nearest eligible VX
futures forward, computes Black--76 implied volatility and Greeks only after monthly
representative selection, and then constructs an expiry holding ledger.

The train window ends on 2020-12-31; all main performance statistics are reported for the
subsequent 60 monthly test observations. Expected returns, covariance estimates, residuals,
betas, state variables, borrow proxies, and cost inputs are read only from training-window
or decision-date information. The gross return is the clean option-only Markowitz object:
selected option payoff or mark return times signed premium/NAV weight. The net research
return subtracts a conservative implementation layer for spreads, explicit fees, slippage,
borrow, capacity, simulated margin funding, and short-option assignment-risk flags. This
net layer is a research simulation, not a broker-executed fill model.

VIX settlement is handled as a gating variable rather than a footnote. A listed VIX option
expiry backtest requires exact VRO/SOQ settlement. The runner accepts exact local VRO/SOQ
files through the configured settlement path. If exact settlement is absent for any VIX
expiry row, the row remains a VIX-close settlement proxy and VIX-enabled results are marked
diagnostic rather than headline-grade. Table~\ref{tab:vixsettlement} is therefore part of
the claim, not just data documentation.

Table~\ref{tab:performance} separates the mathematical portfolio object from the economic
implementation test. The gross option strategies can look attractive before costs. The
post-cost research simulation is deliberately stricter: wide option spreads, capacity
penalties, margin drag, and assignment-risk flags can erase the apparent Sharpe. This is a
useful publication result. It shows that covariance optimization and conditional option
premia are necessary for an option-only Markowitz paper, but they are not sufficient for a
tradable strategy claim.

The regression is an audit, not a causal proof. If the option book is only a levered
large-cap equity book, SPY and single-name betas should explain it. If it is only a
volatility bet, VX, VIX, and VVIX controls should reveal that. The paper therefore reports
alpha, beta, residual volatility, and $R^2$ for every strategy and benchmark. A high point
estimate without a credible HAC t-statistic or without robustness to costs is not treated
as an economic discovery.

The robustness message is intentionally conservative. VIX options are not treated as a
free return source; they are volatility derivatives whose settlement must be exact before
headline claims are allowed. Cost, capacity, margin, and assignment diagnostics determine
whether the gross research edge survives implementation. Appendix tables report the full
trading-data audit, exposure diagnostics, VIX-regime splits, leave-one-out tests, rolling
OOS check, and full inference ledger.

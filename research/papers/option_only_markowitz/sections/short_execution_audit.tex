\section{Execution audit at observed quotes}

The licensed-quote audit changes only spread evidence. It preserves positions, returns,
non-spread costs, integer decisions, and survival gates. The mid scenario uses observed
midpoints while retaining the frozen non-spread terms. The touch scenario substitutes
observed entry and exit spreads. The worst scenario uses the entry-window maximum spread
and the observed exit spread.

Months without observed quotes keep the frozen modeled cost. The audit counts them and
never imputes them. Reconstructing modeled cost from the frozen components leaves a
maximum absolute gap below \(10^{-16}\). The scenario ladder therefore isolates the effect
of quote-based spread evidence.

\subsection{Why the eight-name book with VIX is the headline}

The spread-source distinction decides which historical result deserves emphasis.
Broad-name and VIX rows lack exact panel CBBO in the frozen cost stack, so those rows use
the inferred proxy. The licensed audit finds wider observed broad-name spreads than the
proxy assumed. Figure~\ref{fig:proxy_coverage} places proxy reliance, observed-quote
coverage, and spread quantiles in one view.

\section{Robustness in brief}

The validation suite separates path fragility from formal inference. CPCV recombines
purged test blocks and preserves the zero-wealth boundary.

Monte Carlo resampling tests return ordering. Refit and reprice exercises repeat
estimation and option repricing under locked cost rules. These simulations serve as
\mbox{path-risk diagnostics only}, not as tradable histories or confirmatory observations.

\section{Capacity, execution limits, and claim boundary}

Capacity is part of the result. Figure~\ref{fig:capacity_spreads} compares deployed gross
with the sum of contract liquidity caps and also records the spread-source mix. The local
contract panel does not support mechanical scaling from roughly \(\$1\) million to
\(\$5\) million NAV.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.94\textwidth]{figures/short_capacity_spread_panel.pdf}
\caption{Capacity and spread-source evidence at the frozen account size. Panel (a)
compares deployed gross with the sum of liquidity caps. Panel (b) shows exact panel CBBO
and inferred-proxy shares.}
\label{fig:capacity_spreads}
\end{figure}

Multi-exchange routing could change available depth. The paper marks routing improvement as
\mbox{not claimed}.
The data contain no routed orders or realized fills. The allocator checks assignment
exposure before execution. Any whole-contract breach fails closed to cash. The paper also
leaves the VIX-40 risk-off arm unscored without complete event quotes. A model price cannot
fill any of these gaps after the fact.

The evidence boundary is therefore narrow. The paper supports one retrospective
development candidate at the tested size and under the frozen source hierarchy. It does
not support unrestricted capacity, live execution quality, or performance outside the
recorded universes.

\section{Freeze protocol and conclusion}

The prospective protocol freezes code, configuration, source hashes, artifact hashes,
and the decision rule before the system scores a future observation. Confirmation requires 36
untouched monthly observations beginning 2026-07-31. A missing quote, failed integer
conversion, or survival breach keeps its original status in the ledger. No later return
can change that status.

The conclusion is simple. The Sharpe Prototype found an attractive later-window path but
failed the March 2020 survival test. The Survival Allocator repaired the model and made
cash admissible. The High Ceiling Allocator preserved those repairs and produced the one
development path that remains credible after the licensed-quote audit. That path is the
paper's candidate, not its proof.

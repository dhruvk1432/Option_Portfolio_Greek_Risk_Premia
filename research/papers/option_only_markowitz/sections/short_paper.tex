\section{Introduction and headline result}

The paper asks one question. Can an option-only allocator survive costs, sparse quotes,
whole-contract execution, and option-specific tail risk while retaining useful historical
performance? The evidence supports only a limited answer. One frozen development
configuration survives the checks in this paper. It is not a live track record and it is
not a confirmatory result.

The research path produced three mechanisms. The Sharpe Prototype chose a high historical
Sharpe direction. The Survival Allocator repaired its covariance, objective, cash rule,
and survival gates. The High Ceiling Allocator kept those repairs while allowing a higher
risk ceiling and a direct-or-abstain whole-contract decision.\footnote{Artifact files and
machine-readable ledgers retain the internal codes E1 (Sharpe Prototype), R1 (Survival
Allocator), and R1.1 (High Ceiling Allocator). The internal codes remain in artifact
filenames and verifier keys so the frozen record stays reproducible.}

Figure~\ref{fig:headline_wealth} shows the headline path. The shaded intervals are cash
abstentions after the direct integer conversion failed a hard constraint. Cash is an
allowed portfolio, not a repaired risky book. The drawdown panel keeps the claim tied to
the path rather than to one ratio.

The portfolio problem begins with mean-variance choice
\citep{markowitz1952portfolio,sharpe1966}. Listed options require different accounting
because premium, collateral, and loss exposure are not the same object. Black's futures
option model supplies the traded forward anchor for VIX options
\citep{black1976commodity}. Existing option-allocation work already combines option
returns, Greeks, and portfolio choice
\citep{faias_santaclara2017,zhao_palomar2018,malamud2014}.

The expected-return channels also have established foundations. Coval and Shumway link
expected option returns to option exposure \citep{coval_shumway2001}. Delta-hedged option
returns reveal compensation for volatility risk \citep{bakshi_kapadia2003}. The variance
risk premium predicts aggregate returns \citep{bollerslev_tauchen_zhou2009}. The validation
design follows the literature on backtest overfitting, multiple testing, and combinatorial
cross-validation \citep{white2000reality,hansen2005spa,bailey2017pbo,
bailey_lopezdeprado2014}. The contribution here is an auditable implementation and a clear
record of where earlier implementations fail.

\section{Theory in brief}

\subsection{Premium accounting}

An option position is a funded cashflow. Let \(C_{i,t}\) be the option mark, \(m_i\) its
multiplier, \(N_{i,t}\) the signed contract count, and \(W_t\) portfolio NAV. Define
\emph{premium weights} by
\[
  w_{i,t}=\frac{N_{i,t}m_iC_{i,t}}{W_t}.
\]
If \(r_{i,t+1}=(C_{i,t+1}-C_{i,t})/C_{i,t}\), the option contribution to the NAV return is

\[
  \frac{\Delta W_{i,t+1}}{W_t}=w_{i,t}r_{i,t+1}.
\]
The multiplier cancels because the weight is already premium normalized. A negative
weight records premium received but does not cap the later short-option liability. Gross
premium, margin, collateral, stress loss, and liquidity must therefore remain separate
controls. Residual NAV stays in cash.

\subsection{Greek-induced covariance}

For each contract, the local return map uses premium-normalized delta, centered quadratic
spot exposure, and vega. Stack the state innovations in \(f_{t+1}\), the fixed Greek
loadings in \(B_t^G\), and the unexplained option return in
\(\varepsilon_{t+1}\). Then
\[
 r_{O,t+1}=\mu_{O,t}+B_t^G f_{t+1}+\varepsilon_{t+1}.
\]
Write
\(
\Omega_t=\operatorname{Var}_t(f_{t+1})
\),
\(
\Gamma_t=\operatorname{Cov}_t(f_{t+1},\varepsilon_{t+1})
\), and
\(
\Sigma_{\varepsilon,t}=\operatorname{Var}_t(\varepsilon_{t+1})
\). The option covariance is
\[
 \Sigma_{O,t}
 =B_t^G\Omega_t(B_t^G)^\top
 +B_t^G\Gamma_t
 +\Gamma_t^\top(B_t^G)^\top
 +\Sigma_{\varepsilon,t}.
\]
The two cross terms generally remain because the code fixes \(B_t^G\) from Greeks. It does
not estimate an ordinary least-squares projection that would force orthogonal residuals.
The implementation estimates the joint covariance of factors and residuals, then applies
the linear map \([B_t^G\ I]\). This construction preserves positive semidefiniteness and
the observed cross block.

For VIX options, the state anchor is the matched VX futures contract rather than equity
spot. VIX and VVIX can describe the state, but they do not replace the traded forward in
the option return map. Settlement evidence must also follow the listed contract rule.

\subsection{Expected return and net utility}

The structural mean routes compensation through the same economic exposures:
\[
 \widehat\mu_{i,t}
 =b^\Delta_{i,t}\widehat\lambda^S_t
 +b^\Gamma_{i,t}\widehat\lambda^{var}_t
 +b^\nu_{i,t}\widehat\lambda^{vol}_t
 +b^{skew}_{i,t}\widehat\lambda^{skew}_t
 +b^{tail}_{i,t}\widehat\lambda^{tail}_t
 +\theta^*_{i,t}.
\]
Skew and tail terms enter the mean and named stress tests. They are not covariance
loadings. The separation avoids pretending that the available panel identifies a larger
Greek exposure map.

The Survival and High Ceiling Allocators solve one signed-position problem. For positive
and negative parts \(w_+\) and \(w_-\), the objective is
\[
 \max_{w\in\mathcal C}
 \left\{
 \widehat\mu_t^\top w
 -c_{+,t}^\top w_+
 -c_{-,t}^\top w_-
 -\frac{\gamma}{2}w^\top\Sigma_{O,t}w
 \right\}.
\]
The asymmetric cost vectors sit inside the objective. The feasible set permits \(w=0\)
and limits gross premium, short premium, volatility, CVaR, named stress loss, margin,
collateral, assignment exposure, Greek concentration, and point-in-time liquidity. A
whole-contract book receives a score only after the implementation checks every gate
again. An infeasible book fails closed to cash.

The original universes contain eight equity names. The larger universes contain 56. The
design evaluates each set with and without VIX options. Contract selection applies maturity,
moneyness, mark, open-interest, volume, and quote-quality filters before optimization.
The same point-in-time panel supplies marks, Greeks, and realized option returns.

The cost stack includes half-spread, fees, direction-specific slippage, short-call borrow,
and funding where applicable. These transaction costs enter both selection and reported
net returns. The stack is asymmetric because buying at the ask and covering a short at
the ask are not mirror images of receiving the bid.

Spread evidence follows a fixed ladder. The eight equity names use exact historical panel
CBBO. Rows outside that panel use an inferred CBBO proxy when no matched historical quote
exists. Broad-name and VIX proxy rows are execution sensitivity, not execution truth.
The licensed-quote audit later tests that distinction directly. VIX settlement requires
the appropriate listed settlement record rather than a convenient close.

The baseline comparison includes ordinary underlying Markowitz and capped naive option
books. The stock baseline asks whether option complexity earns its place. The capped naive
book asks whether estimation adds value beyond simple option diversification under the
same costs and capacity rules.

\section{The Survival Allocator}

The Survival Allocator repairs the earlier objective and acceptance rule. It retains the
factor and residual covariance cross terms. It prices predictable costs inside utility.
It allows cash and removes offsetting split-leg gross. It checks CVaR, stress, margin,
collateral, assignment, liquidity, and integer feasibility before accepting a position.

architecture.

\section{The High Ceiling Allocator}

The High Ceiling Allocator raises the predicted-volatility ceiling to 25 percent. It
pursues a 50 percent gross target only along positive-edge directions that the cost-aware
first stage selects. It never forces that target through an infeasible book. The
continuous target converts directly to whole contracts by truncating toward cash. A
feasible conversion trades. An infeasible conversion abstains.

The comparison in Figure~\ref{fig:model_progression} needs an explicit credibility
warning. The prototype's large line begins after its March 2020 zero-absorption failure,
so the common window excludes the event that invalidates it. Researchers also selected
the prototype after inspecting that window. The inferred CBBO proxy supplies its broad
spread evidence. The High Ceiling line is smaller because it belongs to the frozen
survival-first policy that the paper can honestly defend.

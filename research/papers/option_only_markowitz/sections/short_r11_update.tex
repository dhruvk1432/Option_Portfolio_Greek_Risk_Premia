\section{R1.1 higher-risk development extension}
\label{sec:r11_update}

R1.1 is a separately versioned development arm. It does not replace the frozen R1
artifacts or convert any observation into confirmatory evidence. The policy raises the
annualized predicted-volatility ceiling from 15 to 25 percent and uses the smallest
positive risk-aversion coefficient consistent with that ceiling. It also records a 50
percent premium-gross target, but does not impose an unconditional gross floor.

The deployment step first solves the ordinary R1 net-utility problem. Only directions
selected by that solution and having positive forecast return after their direction-specific
cost are eligible for expansion. Holding those signs fixed makes gross linear and prevents
offsetting long/short legs in the same contract. The implementation tests whether a
50 percent book satisfies volatility, CVaR, stress, margin, collateral, liquidity,
assignment, and concentration limits. If not, it keeps the first-stage book. Thus
``target'' is not a euphemism for compulsory investment.

Whole-contract execution follows a direct-or-abstain policy. Contract counts in the
continuous target are truncated toward zero and the resulting book is rechecked against
every hard constraint. If it is feasible, that is the only risky portfolio eligible for
execution. If it is infeasible, the strategy holds cash for that period. It does not
remove a risk-producing leg, add a VIX hedge, iteratively shrink the book, or substitute a
mixed-integer optimum, because those would be different portfolios from the target the
continuous model selected. A cash abstention is therefore a valid trading decision rather
than an integer-execution failure.

The artifact ledger records both the attempted direct conversion and the selected cash
book. Headline exposure and risk diagnostics correctly equal zero during an abstention,
while separate fields retain the rejected conversion's CVaR, stress, volatility, margin,
collateral, assignment, liquidity-cap, and base-constraint values. Thus cash reporting no
longer erases the reason the target was not traded.

\subsection{VIX-40 close-to-next-open rule}

The risk-off state changes only after an official VIX close. A close at or above 40 sends
an exit order at the first executable option quote after the next regular-session open;
the first later close below 40 sends a re-entry attempt on the same timing convention.
The market observations remain in all later covariance, premia, and EGARCH training
windows while the strategy is in cash.

March 1, 2020 was a Sunday. The user reports having decided on that date to exit, but this
statement is retrospective and is not generalized to all traders. The VIX had closed at
40.11 on February 28, so the threshold rule and the user-attested instruction both map to
Monday, March 2. The event ledger merges them into one order. The March 2 VIX close of
33.42 maps to a March 3 re-entry attempt. Later threshold crossings are handled by the same
rule rather than being selectively omitted.

The repository does not presently contain matched March 2/3 and later event-date CBBO for
every held equity option. R1.1 therefore generates a licensed-data request manifest and
does not score the risk-off return. Long liquidations require executable bids, short covers
require executable asks, and displayed size is consumed sequentially. Missing or incomplete
fills cannot be replaced by a model price or by the no-rule return. Consequently the
risk-off arm is an implemented but currently unscored execution experiment.

\subsection{EGARCH diagnostic}

The optional diagnostic fits an EGARCH(1,1) model with Student innovations to daily
underlying and VX-forward returns using only observations available by each decision date.
Its 21-day variance ratio is clipped to $[0.5,2]$. Delta-factor standard deviations are
scaled by the forecast volatility ratio and centered-quadratic factors by the corresponding
variance ratio; vega and residual scales remain one. Applying one diagonal transformation
to the complete joint factor/residual covariance preserves positive semidefiniteness and
the factor/residual cross terms.

Promotion required at least 95 percent forecast coverage, at least a 2 percent pooled QLIKE
improvement with a favorable 90 percent block-bootstrap interval, no added survival
failure, and no universe-level expected-shortfall deterioration above one NAV percentage
point. The checked run achieved 99.9 percent forecast coverage but materially worsened
QLIKE and worsened the worst universe's expected shortfall by 1.51 percentage points.
EGARCH therefore remains a diagnostic and is not part of the R1.1 candidate.

The 25 percent ceiling increased risk but did not make 50 percent premium gross feasible:
the target-hit rate is zero in every universe. Direct conversion is feasible and selected
in 621 of the 744 base/EGARCH decisions. The other 123 decisions are explicit cash
abstentions: 57 in the two original+VIX arms and 66 in the two larger+VIX arms; neither
non-VIX arm abstains. The attempted book's original breach values remain reported for all
123 decisions. No substitute risky portfolio contributes to returns, and abstention is not
counted as an integer-execution or survival failure.

For the non-EGARCH R1.1 arm, annualized geometric return is 11.77 percent in the original
universe, 12.28 percent in the larger universe, 16.01 percent with original+VIX, and 14.23
percent with larger+VIX. The corresponding maximum drawdowns are 38.11, 39.40, 17.05, and
12.13 percent. The two non-VIX books never abstain; original+VIX holds cash in 32 of 93
months and larger+VIX in 34 of 93 months. These remain retrospective development results,
not confirmatory evidence, and the no-trade convention is part of the newly versioned
R1.1 policy rather than a reinterpretation of E1.

These results do not alter the original E1 diagnosis. The legacy VIX-enabled CPCV paths
remain absorbed at zero in March 2020 and continue to fail the survival gate. The unscored
VIX-40 arm cannot be used to overwrite those paths. A separate R1.1 freeze manifest records
the new policy, source hashes, data cutoff, and a fresh requirement of 36 untouched monthly
observations before confirmation.
